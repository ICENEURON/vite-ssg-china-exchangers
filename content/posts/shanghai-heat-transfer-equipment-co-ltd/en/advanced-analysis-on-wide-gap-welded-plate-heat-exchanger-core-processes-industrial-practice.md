Wide-gap welded plate heat exchangers are built for a difficult corner of process engineering: services where the fluid is valuable, abrasive, viscous, fiber-rich, or quick to foul. In these duties, conventional gasketed plate heat exchangers may transfer heat efficiently but clog too easily, while shell-and-tube equipment may keep flowing but require a larger footprint and more alloy material.

Shanghai Heat Transfer Equipment Co., Ltd. positions its wide-gap welded plate heat exchanger (WGPHE) as a bridge between those two extremes. The design combines wide free-flow passages with a fully welded plate pack so the unit can handle slurry, pulp, process liquor, and other difficult streams without relying on elastomer gaskets.

## Why Wide-Gap Design Matters

The main engineering problem is not only heat transfer. It is keeping heat transfer stable over time. High-solid alumina liquor, black liquor, sugar syrup, ethanol mash, sludge, and similar fluids can deposit on heat transfer surfaces or collect at narrow contact points.

SHPHE's wide-gap approach opens the channel spacing to roughly 8 mm to 30 mm depending on the duty. That physical clearance gives particles and fibers more room to pass. Plate patterns such as dimpled or column-supported channels add controlled disturbance without creating the dense contact network found in ordinary gasketed plate exchangers.

## Heat Transfer Under Dirty Conditions

Wide channels do not automatically mean weak heat transfer. The plate geometry is used to create secondary flows, vortex shedding, and wall shear that disturb the thermal boundary layer. For high-solid slurry, this helps suspend particles and reduce wall deposition. For viscous or non-Newtonian fluids, the induced mixing improves contact between the bulk fluid and the plate surface.

This is the core process advantage: the exchanger is not optimized only for clean-water test conditions. It is shaped around the actual flow behavior of industrial fluids that scale, settle, thicken, or carry fibers.

## Welding and Structural Reliability

The wide-gap plate pack is fully welded, eliminating the gasket as a limiting component. SHPHE highlights resistance seam welding as a manufacturing method for creating continuous, consistent joints along the channel edges. For hazardous or high-value media, this welded construction reduces leakage points and lowers the need for routine gasket replacement.

The welded structure also supports more demanding service windows than gasketed equipment. The source material describes wide-gap welded designs for temperatures up to about 350 C and pressures up to about 3.5 MPa, subject to project-specific design and material selection.

## Material Strategy

Because many wide-gap duties involve corrosion or abrasion, material selection is part of the thermal design. SHPHE references stainless steels, duplex grades, nickel alloys, and titanium for different operating environments.

- Duplex stainless steels can support high-chloride or abrasive slurry applications.
- Nickel alloys such as Hastelloy grades are relevant for aggressive chemical services.
- Titanium is useful where oxidizing media or seawater corrosion resistance is required.

The value of the compact welded-plate form becomes clearer when high-alloy materials are involved: less surface area and lower equipment volume can reduce total material consumption compared with large tubular units.

## Typical Industrial Practice

Common WGPHE applications include alumina slurry cooling, pulp and paper heat recovery, sugar process heating, fuel ethanol mash cooling, wastewater concentration, and chemical process heat recovery. These services share a common requirement: the exchanger must continue operating even when the fluid is not clean, thin, or uniform.

For buyers, the practical question is not simply whether the equipment can meet the initial heat load. It is whether the heat exchanger can maintain performance between cleaning cycles, reduce shutdown frequency, and remain serviceable when fouling eventually occurs.

## Takeaway

SHPHE's wide-gap welded plate heat exchanger is best understood as a reliability-focused plate heat exchanger. It keeps the compactness and thermal intensity of plate technology while redesigning the channel, weld, and material system around slurry and fouling-prone media.

For process plants handling high-solid, high-viscosity, or fiber-containing fluids, that combination can reduce lifecycle cost more effectively than a simple clean-service efficiency comparison.
