Alumina decomposition and precipitation cooling place unusual stress on heat exchangers. Process liquor can be alkaline, hot, particle-laden, and prone to crystallization. If the exchanger channel is too narrow, scaling and particle accumulation shorten operating cycles. If the equipment is oversized to avoid plugging, energy efficiency and installation economics suffer.
SHPHE's wide-gap welded plate heat exchanger is designed for this tradeoff. The unit uses a welded plate bundle with wider flow passages, allowing it to process slurry and crystallizing media while retaining the compact heat transfer behavior associated with plate-type equipment.

## Operating Challenges in Alumina Service

In alumina precipitation, temperature control affects crystal growth and product quality. The cooling system must therefore remove heat steadily while tolerating media that can scale or carry suspended solids.

Traditional plate heat exchangers may deliver high heat transfer coefficients, but their narrow channels and dense contact points can create blockage risk. Shell-and-tube exchangers tolerate larger particles better, yet they normally require more space, more metal, and more difficult cleaning work.

## SHPHE's Wide-Gap Solution

The wide-gap welded plate design combines several features relevant to alumina service:

- A fully welded plate pack reduces gasket-related leakage risk.
- Dimpled and studded plate patterns form wider flow passages for slurry.
- Detachable cold and hot side structures can be specified to simplify mechanical cleaning.
- Vertical or horizontal installation can be selected around site layout and slurry behavior.

The broader channel geometry helps reduce plugging while still promoting turbulence near the heat transfer surface. This matters because alumina duty is not only about passing solids through the exchanger; it is also about preventing stagnant pockets where scale can start growing.

## Buyer Considerations

For alumina plant engineers, the purchasing decision should focus on the full cleaning and reliability profile, not only initial heat duty. Important review points include slurry velocity, particle size distribution, scale tendency, cleaning access, allowable pressure drop, plate material, and pressure vessel compliance.

When those factors are addressed together, wide-gap welded plate heat exchangers can reduce the gap between compact thermal design and dependable slurry operation.

## Takeaway

SHPHE's alumina decomposition application shows how wide-gap welded plate technology can be adapted to heavy industrial media. The value proposition is straightforward: maintain compact heat transfer while giving slurry and crystallizing fluids enough channel space, structural reliability, and cleaning access to operate for long cycles.
