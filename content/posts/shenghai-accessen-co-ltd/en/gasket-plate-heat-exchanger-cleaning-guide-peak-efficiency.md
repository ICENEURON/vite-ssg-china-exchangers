Gasketed plate heat exchangers are efficient because they place fluids close to each other across thin corrugated plates. The same compact geometry also means that fouling, gasket damage, or incorrect tightening can quickly affect performance. A structured cleaning process helps restore heat duty while reducing the risk of leaks after reassembly.

## 1. Prepare the Work Area

Before opening the exchanger, isolate the unit from pressure, heat, electrical sources, and any connected pump system. Drain the fluids safely and confirm that the equipment has cooled to a serviceable temperature. Prepare soft brushes, non-abrasive pads, approved cleaning chemicals, clean water, a torque wrench, lifting support if required, and personal protective equipment.

Record the current tightening dimension, flow arrangement, plate sequence, gasket condition, and visible leakage marks. Photos taken before disassembly can save time during reassembly.

## 2. Disassemble Carefully

Loosen frame bolts gradually and evenly, following a diagonal sequence. Opening one side too quickly can bend plates or overload the frame. As plates are removed, keep them in order and mark any plates with visible corrosion, deformation, or damaged gaskets.

Gaskets should be handled gently. If a gasket has hardened, swollen, cracked, or separated from the groove, cleaning alone will not solve the problem; replacement is normally the better choice.

## 3. Clean Plates and Gaskets

Start with water rinsing to remove loose debris. Then use a soft brush or non-abrasive pad for deposits that remain on the plate surface. Avoid steel brushes or aggressive scraping because scratches can become future fouling points or corrosion initiation sites.

For mineral scale, weak organic acids such as citric acid may be suitable depending on the plate material. For oil or biological films, alkaline cleaning may be more effective. Strong acids and chlorides should be avoided unless the material and procedure have been verified by the equipment supplier.

After chemical cleaning, rinse thoroughly until the surface is neutral and free of residue. Inspect each plate for pinholes, cracks, gasket groove damage, and deformation.

## 4. Reassemble and Test

Reinstall plates in the recorded order and orientation. Make sure gaskets are seated properly and are not twisted or stretched. Tighten bolts gradually in a cross pattern until the required tightening dimension is reached. Over-tightening can deform plates, while under-tightening can cause leakage.

Begin with a low-pressure water test. If no leakage appears, raise pressure step by step toward the required test or operating pressure. Watch for external leakage, abnormal frame movement, and unusual pressure loss.

## 5. Prevent Repeat Fouling

Cleaning should be paired with prevention. Review water hardness, solids loading, filtration, flow velocity, temperature program, and chemical compatibility. For critical systems, trend pressure drop and temperature approach so the next cleaning can be scheduled by performance evidence rather than guesswork.

A clean gasketed plate heat exchanger is not only more efficient. It is also easier to inspect, easier to operate, and less likely to surprise the plant with a leak during peak production.
